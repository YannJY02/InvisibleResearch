review, internship report, or other formal project writing. Apply the relevant
requirements even when they are absent from the user's latest prompt.

## Before and during writing

1. Identify the target document and audience from the task and existing project
   context. Read its owner's requirements index if present. For a new formal
   target, inspect the official or supplied requirements before drafting the
   sections that depend on them.
   For research/progress/meeting presentation reports, also read
   [research report requirements](research-report-requirements.md) on every
   creation or revision and use its delivery checklist before handing back the result.
2. Separate binding institutional/venue rules, supervisor instructions,
   optional guidance, and unconfirmed requirements. Keep exact source locators
   for rules about structure, length, language, citation style, layout, required
   declarations, and submission filename. Check current-year/version details
   when relevant; do not infer them from old templates or memory.
3. If an essential rule or target is missing or contradictory, state the
   concrete gap and ask for that decision or inaccessible source. Continue
   independent research or provisional drafting with assumptions labelled.
   Do not claim submission readiness while a mandatory rule remains unresolved.
4. Review the draft against each applicable rule and inspect the rendered
   output when formatting is part of delivery. Record what was checked and
   any unresolved rule in the owner's existing requirements index or delivery
   note. No separate checklist file is needed for routine prose changes.

## Register a new target

Use `papers/<paper>/requirements/README.md` as the target-specific entry point.
For each requirement record: issuer, target/output, version or year, status
(`binding`, `supervisor`, `guidance`, `unconfirmed`), exact source URL or approved
local copy, page/section, rule, affected section, and verification method.
Preserve an original source's bytes; put extraction and interpretation in the
index. Store private originals in the approved private location and link a
non-sensitive locator instead of committing them.

For a reusable official form, retain its required structure and terminology.
For scientific prose, preserve the distinction between plans and observed
results, and between reproducibility and authorization to support a paper
claim. Requirement compliance does not create a Paper Analysis designation.
